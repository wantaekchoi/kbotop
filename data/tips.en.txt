# kbotop bottom-bar tips (English) — one tip per line. '#' lines and blank lines are ignored.
# Plain statements of baseball rules, stats, and KBO conventions, written from scratch.
# Keep every line under 75 display columns (a test enforces this).

Three strikes is a strikeout; four balls is a walk.
The strike zone spans home plate, from the knees to the mid-torso.
A foul counts as a strike, but never as the third one.
A runner off the base is out on a tag; on a force, the base tag does it.
The pitcher must throw inside the pitch clock or a ball is added.
ERA is the runs a pitcher allows per nine innings, errors excluded.
WHIP is walks plus hits allowed, divided by innings pitched.
Nine innings; a tie goes to extras.
The home team bats last, in the bottom of each inning.
A sacrifice fly scores a runner and is not charged to the average.
The designated hitter bats for the pitcher and never fields.
A double play retires two runners on one batted ball.
The count reads balls first: "2-1" is two balls, one strike.
KBO plays 144 games; the top five reach the postseason.
Runners left on base still charge to the pitcher who put them there.
A balk is an illegal pitching motion; every runner advances a base.
An infield fly is an automatic out with runners on and under two outs.
A hold goes to a reliever who protects a lead without finishing.
Pitch types here come from the tracking data in the broadcast feed.
A quality start is six innings or more with three earned runs or fewer.
On a dropped third strike the batter may run if first base is open.
A ground-rule double is a fair ball that bounces over the fence.
An intentional walk puts a batter on first to set up a better matchup.
A player replaced by a pinch hitter or runner cannot return that game.
A starter needs five innings and the lead to be in line for the win.
A blown save is a lead lost in a save situation.
A wild pitch is charged to the pitcher; a passed ball to the catcher.
Earned runs exclude anything that scored because of an error.
On-base percentage counts walks and hit-by-pitch, not just hits.
Slugging is total bases per at-bat, so extra-base hits drive it up.
OPS is on-base plus slugging, a quick read on total offense.
Scoring position means a runner on second or third.
An infield hit is a ground ball the batter beats out for a single.
An error is a play a fielder should have made, letting a runner live.
Fielder's choice is when the defense goes after another runner instead.
The cycle is a single, double, triple, and homer in one game.
A no-hitter allows no hits; a perfect game allows no baserunners at all.
A complete game goes the distance; a shutout adds no runs allowed.
A squeeze play bunts the runner home from third.
Hit and run sends the runner while the batter must protect with a swing.
A bunt is a soft tap that rolls the ball short of the fielders.
Bunting foul with two strikes is a strikeout.
On a check swing the plate umpire can ask the base umpire for help.
A runner hit by a batted ball before it passes a fielder is out.
Running more than three feet out of the lane to dodge a tag is an out.
The catcher may not block the plate without the ball.
A foul tip caught cleanly by the catcher is a strike.
The batting order is fixed; a substitute inherits the same slot.
A productive out still moves the runner up a base.
With the bases loaded, a walk or hit-by-pitch forces in a run.
A game called early still counts once five innings are in the books.
A suspended game picks up later from where it stopped.
Replay review can overturn calls like safe or out.
Win probability here is a live model estimate, not a prediction.
Whether watching in person helps the team is a matter of faith.
Every fan base swears the team loses whenever they tune in.
Ordering fried chicken is said to start the comeback. Results vary.
The real game starts with two outs in the ninth, or so they say.
It ain't over till it's over.
Nobody mentions a no-hitter while it is still going.
Change the channel and your team scores. Change it back and they stop.
If the team only wins when you wear the jersey, that is scouting now.
Magic numbers are best counted only when they go down.
Players on a hot streak rarely change anything about their day.
A team's uniform number retires with the player, not the season.
Batting average tells you contact; OPS tells you damage.
A save needs a lead of three or fewer, or the tying run on deck.
Pitchers hit the showers; catchers hit the ice pack.
The seventh-inning stretch is when the ballpark remembers it is a party.
The bullpen is both a place and a state of mind.
A rain delay is the only time the tarp gets an ovation.
